Timelines & expectations

How long does SEO take to show results?–

Technical fixes can move rankings within four to six weeks. Content and authority compound more slowly — most clients see meaningful traffic growth by month three and clear revenue impact by month six. Anyone promising page one in 30 days is selling something else.

Do you guarantee first-page rankings?+

No, and nobody can honestly. Rankings depend on competitors, algorithm changes and your site's history — none of which any agency controls. What we guarantee is the work, the reporting and the transparency. The forecast in your audit shows every assumption, so you can judge the likelihood yourself.

What happens if Google releases a major update?+

We monitor rankings daily across every client, so we usually spot volatility before the update is confirmed. You get a written briefing within 72 hours explaining what changed, whether it affected you, and what we're adjusting. Roadmap changes triggered by an update are never billed as extra scope.

Working together

Do you require a long contract?+

Three months minimum, because that's the honest amount of time the work needs to land. After that it's 30-day rolling. No annual lock-in, and your roadmap, documentation and data all go with you if you leave.

Do you implement changes or just advise?+

We implement. Our engineers work directly in your codebase or CMS wherever access allows. If your dev team prefers to own deploys, we hand over ticket-ready specifications and review the release before it ships — no vague "improve site speed" notes left for someone else to interpret.

Do you work alongside our existing agency or in-house team?+

Often, yes. We frequently act as the technical and strategic layer alongside an in-house content team, or as the SEO specialists inside a broader paid-media relationship. We'll flag overlaps honestly rather than quietly duplicate spend.

What do you need from us to get started?+

Read access to GA4 and Search Console, a staging or CMS login where possible, and one internal owner who can approve content and prioritise dev tickets. That last one matters more than people expect — engagements stall on approval bottlenecks far more often than on strategy.

Methods & reporting

How do you build links?+

Digital PR, original data studies, expert commentary and genuine editorial outreach. No private blog networks, no paid link marketplaces, no directory spam — the tactics that get sites penalised are the ones we spend our time cleaning up for new clients.

What does reporting actually look like?+

A live dashboard combining GA4, Search Console and rank data, plus a written monthly summary tied to leads and revenue rather than impressions. You also get a fortnightly call with the strategist who does the work, not an account manager relaying it. All data stays exportable and yours.

Which industries do you work with?+

Mostly B2B SaaS, e-commerce and multi-location service brands. We decline work in sectors where we'd have to compromise on link ethics or content accuracy, and we won't take a second client competing directly with an existing one in the same market.
